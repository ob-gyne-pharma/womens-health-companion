Facing a cancer investigation or diagnosis

This is a preparation guide, not a cancer treatment recommendation.

A guide to understand and prepare

When to seek care

If you are already receiving treatment, use your oncology team’s urgent-contact instructions. Severe symptoms need urgent care.

Urgent-help guide
Understand the concern

An investigation is not the same as a confirmed diagnosis. Ask what is known, what remains uncertain and which result will clarify the next step. Cancer care differs by organ, type and stage.

What assessment may involve

For example, ovarian cancer treatment may involve surgery and chemotherapy; some people receive targeted medicines. A specialist team considers the diagnosis and personal situation. This example must not be applied to other cancers or used to choose your treatment.

Care options to discuss

Take a trusted person if you wish and request a written plan. Ask about treatment goals, side effects, fertility, sexual health and symptom support. Clarify who coordinates care and when you should receive results. You may ask about a second opinion.

These are discussion points, not a prescription. Suitability and availability vary.

A question you may have

Does this guide decide my treatment?

No. It helps you prepare for a qualified service and understand what to ask.

Prepare for your appointment

What to record

Bring your main question and any previous results you want to discuss. Share sensitive details only where you feel safe.

Questions worth asking

  • What is confirmed, and what are the goals and alternatives of treatment?
